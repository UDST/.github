The **Urban Data Science Toolkit (UDST)** maintains open-source software for urban simulation, transportation and accessibility analysis, and discrete-choice modeling.

The projects originated in research at the University of Washington and UC Berkeley. They have since been used by researchers, students, public agencies, consultants, and software developers.

## Projects

| Project | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **[UrbanSim](https://github.com/UDST/urbansim)** | Microsimulation of urban development, real-estate markets, household and employment location, and the effects of transportation and land-use policy. |
| **[Orca](https://github.com/UDST/orca)** | A workflow framework for organizing data, derived variables, model steps, and dependencies in data-driven simulations. |
| **[Pandana](https://github.com/UDST/pandana)** | High-performance network accessibility, aggregation, nearest-point-of-interest, and shortest-path analysis. |
| **[ChoiceModels](https://github.com/UDST/choicemodels)** | Estimation and simulation of discrete-choice models, including location-choice applications. |
| **[UrbanAccess](https://github.com/UDST/urbanaccess)** | Construction and analysis of multimodal urban transportation networks. |

The organization also retains historical and experimental repositories when they are useful for understanding earlier models or reproducing published work.

## UrbanSim in practice

UrbanSim has been used in peer-reviewed research, agency modeling, and independent implementations in North America, Latin America, Europe, Africa, Asia, and Australia.

Published and publicly documented applications include:

- regional land-use and transportation modeling in **Puget Sound, Phoenix, Austin, San Francisco, Salt Lake City, Brussels, Paris, Zürich, and Tel Aviv**;
- housing, employment, and real-estate applications in **Lyon, Seoul, Suwon, and St. Gallen**;
- cross-domain research connecting urban development with **water demand, precipitation, energy, accessibility, and transportation**; and
- adaptations for rapidly changing and data-constrained urban regions, including **Greater Accra, Johannesburg, eThekwini, Nelson Mandela Bay, Tshwane, and Ekurhuleni**.

Work in Latin America includes a known deployment in **Ciudad Juárez**, an initial **Bogotá–Cundinamarca** model commissioned by the World Bank, and published research assessing the feasibility of applying UrbanSim in **São Paulo**. These projects reached different stages of development and should not all be described as operational deployments.

Additional historical deployments include models developed with metropolitan, state, and national organizations in the United States, Canada, Australia, and Mexico.

Applications vary in purpose and maturity. Some became operational agency systems; others were research prototypes, model components, or historical implementations. Inclusion in the application history does not imply endorsement or a current commercial relationship.

## Research foundation

UrbanSim and related UDST projects have received public research support since 1999. The principal awards include:

- six National Science Foundation projects focused on urban simulation, software architecture, uncertainty, public participation, artificial intelligence, and visualization;
- an EPA Science to Achieve Results grant integrating land use, transportation, and air-quality modeling; and
- additional investment from research councils, foundations, universities, and transportation and planning agencies.

Related NSF Biocomplexity projects connected UrbanSim with research on land-cover change, real-estate development, and urban ecosystems.

Later research administered through UrbanSim Inc. includes DOE-supported integrated modeling work with Lawrence Berkeley and Argonne national laboratories, a multi-phase CMHC program on Canadian housing markets, and a HUD Office of Policy Development and Research award on alternative methods for forecasting Fair Market Rents. These company-administered awards and subcontracts are documented separately from academic research grants and from the open-source UDST repositories.

## Project status and contributing

UDST includes actively maintained projects, stable research software, and historical repositories retained for reproducibility. Current priorities and repository status are tracked in the **[UDST Maintenance Revival project](https://github.com/orgs/UDST/projects/1)**.

Community participation is welcome. Before beginning substantial work, please review the organization-wide **[contribution guidelines](https://github.com/UDST/.github/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)** and the guidance in the relevant repository.

## Open source and supported implementations

UDST repositories provide open-source foundations for research, education, experimentation, and technical development.

**[UrbanSim Inc.](https://www.urbansim.com/)** provides commercial implementation, hosting, data preparation, calibration, training, and support.

Open-source use does not imply endorsement of UrbanSim Inc. or a commercial relationship. Commercial customers, research collaborators, and independent open-source users are identified separately in public application materials.

## Stewardship and governance

The UDST GitHub organization is owned and administered by **Paul Waddell**, who created UrbanSim and has led its research and development across its academic, public-sector, open-source, and commercial phases. Repository maintainers and contributors provide the technical work and review that sustain the individual projects.

Administrative ownership is distinct from copyright in individual contributions. Each repository remains governed by its published open-source license. Funding or participating in a project does not confer ownership of UDST or control of a repository.
