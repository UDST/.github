# Research support and acknowledgments

> **Publication draft.** Award information was extracted from Paul Waddell's 2023 academic CV. Items marked for verification must be reconciled with authoritative sponsor records before this page is published.

UrbanSim and the Urban Data Science Toolkit grew from a research program led continuously by Paul Waddell, as principal investigator or co-principal investigator, beginning in 1999. Universities, research sponsors, collaborators, and planning agencies funded different parts of the modeling system and its regional applications.

This page recognizes that support while distinguishing among:

- **competitive research awards** supporting scientific and software development;
- **agency-sponsored projects** supporting research or regional implementations; and
- **company-administered engagements** supporting particular customers or production platforms.

The categories are not combined into a single total. Some entries report the full award while others report only the share received by UC Berkeley or the University of Washington. The list also includes both U.S. dollars and euros.

## Department of Energy-supported national laboratory work

UrbanSim Inc. has contributed to U.S. Department of Energy research through subcontracts with Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and Argonne National Laboratory. This work was administered through the company, not as an academic grant to Paul Waddell or UDST. Contract values are therefore omitted.

### Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory

Under DOE's Vehicle Technologies Office and SMART Mobility program, UrbanSim Inc. supported the integration of UrbanSim with LBNL's Behavior, Energy, Autonomy, and Mobility (BEAM) modeling system. The reviewed scopes cover:

- coordination, management, and software development for BEAM–UrbanSim integration under SMART Mobility 2.0;
- connecting BEAM to UrbanSim regional applications, initially including Austin and Detroit;
- providing model access, technical support, and training through the UrbanCanvas Modeler environment;
- collaborative scenario analysis, indicators, and visualization of linked land-use, transportation, freight, energy, and emissions outcomes; and
- subsequent BEAM CORE application and deployment work involving the San Francisco Bay Area and Puget Sound, including zero-emission freight strategies, regional planning workflows, and integration with planning-agency models.

The later agreement describes UrbanSim Inc.'s role as research and development support for energy-efficient-mobility integrated modeling and application development. LBNL remained the prime laboratory and project lead.

### Argonne National Laboratory

Current Argonne statements of work engage UrbanSim Inc. in integrated land-use, transportation, and building-energy modeling for DOE Vehicle Technologies Office research. The work links UrbanSim with Argonne's POLARIS transportation model and LBNL's CityBES building-energy system. The reviewed scope includes:

- access to calibrated UrbanSim models for Chicago and Austin;
- API and data-exchange support for the UrbanSim–POLARIS–CityBES workflow;
- recalibration and sensitivity testing using travel-time and travel-cost inputs;
- scenario controls for transit-oriented development, telecommuting, and private autonomous vehicles; and
- analysis of land-use policy, freight, transportation-demand management, and cross-sector energy effects.

The statements of work identify Paul Waddell as the UrbanSim Inc. project manager. They refer to DOE Vehicle Technologies Office projects concerned with regional transportation pathways and integrated transportation-and-energy systems.

## Company-administered public-sector engagements

CMHC and HUD supported later work through UrbanSim Inc. or its predecessor companies rather than through academic research grants. These engagements should not be included in academic-grant counts or totals.

### CMHC housing-market modeling program

Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation engaged UrbanSim Inc. in a multi-phase housing research and modeling program beginning in 2017 and continuing through at least 2022. The work began with a detailed behavioral simulation model for Metro Vancouver and expanded to Toronto and the Greater Golden Horseshoe, national model-ready data, and a Canada-wide data viewer.

The reviewed scopes include:

- synthetic household, employment, parcel, building, rent, price, and transportation-network data at detailed geographic resolution;
- residential and employment location models, price and rent equilibration, household budget constraints, and real-estate development feasibility;
- calibration, validation, sensitivity testing, and long-range simulation of housing-market and development outcomes;
- macroeconomic components linking housing prices, rents, income, and employment over time;
- zoning data and tools supporting housing-supply and development-feasibility analysis;
- national data visualization, scenario indicators, documentation, and training for researchers and public agencies; and
- continued model refinement and access to the UrbanCanvas Modeler environment.

The program combined UrbanSim's behavioral modeling methods with company-developed data and cloud-platform capabilities. It should therefore be presented as substantive public-sector research and model implementation administered by UrbanSim Inc., not as funding for the open-source UDST repositories. Contract values are intentionally omitted.

### HUD Fair Market Rent research

In FY2020, the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development's Office of Policy Development and Research selected UrbanSim Inc.'s proposal, *Alternative Methods for Calculating Fair Market Rents in Rental Markets with Rapidly Rising Rents*, for an award. Paul Waddell was the principal investigator and UrbanSim Inc. was the award recipient; the Terner Center for Housing Innovation participated as a research partner.

The research examined methods for improving the trend component used to forecast HUD Fair Market Rents and for identifying rental markets experiencing unusually rapid increases. The work included multivariate time-series and cointegration models, evaluation of alternative rental-market data, statistical analysis of rapidly rising rents, an expert review process, and policy recommendations. UrbanSim Inc. and the Terner Center produced the final technical report in 2022.

This was a federal research award to UrbanSim Inc., not an academic grant to Paul Waddell or UDST. The award amount is intentionally omitted.

## Suggested acknowledgment

> UrbanSim and the Urban Data Science Toolkit grew from a research program led by Paul Waddell over more than two decades, with many academic and agency collaborators. Waddell served as principal investigator or co-principal investigator on six core National Science Foundation projects, an EPA Science to Achieve Results grant, two related NSF Biocomplexity projects, and additional awards from research sponsors and planning agencies.
