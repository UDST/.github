# Research support and acknowledgments

UrbanSim and the Urban Data Science Toolkit developed through a research
program led by Paul Waddell and sustained by academic collaborators, public
research sponsors, planning agencies, and later work through UrbanSim Inc.
This page records major sources of that support and recognizes the people and
institutions that contributed to the work.

The entries below distinguish competitive research awards from agency-sponsored
implementations and company-administered research. Funding acknowledgment does
not imply current sponsorship or endorsement of UDST, its projects, or UrbanSim
Inc.

### Related NSF Biocomplexity research

Two broader NSF projects connected this research program with land-cover
change, real-estate development, urban ecosystems, and biodiversity:

- [BCS-0120024](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=0120024),
  *BE/CNH: Modeling Interactions Among Urban Development, Land-Cover Change,
  and Bird Diversity* (2001–2006; $1,128,818). Marina Alberti was PI; John
  Marzluff, Paul Waddell, and Mark Handcock were co-PIs.
- [BCS-0508002](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=0508002),
  *BE/CNH: Urban Landscape Patterns: Complex Dynamics and Emergent Properties*
  (2005–2010; $1,448,862). This was a related University of Washington
  collaboration led by Marina Alberti. The NSF award record does not list Paul
  Waddell as a PI or co-PI, so it is not included among the six awards above.

## EPA Science to Achieve Results

EPA STAR award [R831837](https://cfpub.epa.gov/ncer_abstracts/index.cfm/fuseaction/display.highlight/abstract_id/7404/report/F),
*Integrating Land Use, Transportation, and Air Quality Modeling* (2004–2008),
supported the integration of land-use, travel-demand, and air-quality models.
Paul Waddell served as principal investigator, with Chandra Bhat, David Layton,
Maren Outwater, and Ram Pendyala as co-principal investigators. The award was
$700,000.

## Later publicly sponsored work through UrbanSim Inc.

Some later research was performed through UrbanSim Inc. rather than through a
university or UDST. It is included here to document continuity in the research
program, not as funding of the open-source repositories. Contract values are
not reported.

### U.S. Department of Energy and national laboratories

Through subcontracts with Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and Argonne
National Laboratory, UrbanSim Inc. contributed land-use modeling to integrated
research sponsored by the U.S. Department of Energy's Vehicle Technologies
Office. Publicly documented work has included integration of UrbanSim with
LBNL's BEAM mobility model and Argonne's POLARIS transportation model. These
efforts examined interactions among land use, transportation, buildings,
energy use, and emissions. The national laboratories remained the prime
contractors and research leads.

See the DOE report on [SMART Mobility modeling workflows](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/vehicles/articles/smart-mobility-modeling-workflow-development-implementation-and-results).

### U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development

HUD's Office of Policy Development and Research selected UrbanSim Inc. to study
alternative methods for calculating Fair Market Rents in markets with rapidly
rising rents. Paul Waddell was principal investigator, and the Terner Center
for Housing Innovation was a research partner. HUD published the resulting
[Fair Market Rents Trend Component Modeling Technical Report](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/publications/Fair-Market-Rents-Trend-Component-Modeling-Technical-Report.html)
in 2023.

### Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation

Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation supported a multi-phase program of
housing-market research and model development through UrbanSim Inc. Work began
with Metro Vancouver and subsequently included Toronto and the Greater Golden
Horseshoe, model-ready national data, and tools for examining housing supply,
prices, rents, development feasibility, and policy scenarios. This was a
company-administered public-sector engagement rather than funding for UDST.
