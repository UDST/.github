# Open-source projects and commercial offerings

UDST and UrbanSim Inc. serve different, complementary purposes.

## UDST

The Urban Data Science Toolkit maintains open-source software for urban simulation, workflow orchestration, network accessibility, discrete-choice modeling, and multimodal network analysis. Its repositories support:

- transparent and reproducible research;
- education and independent experimentation;
- self-managed implementations by public agencies, researchers, consultants, and developers;
- maintenance of established APIs and methods; and
- community contributions that fit the purpose and long-term capacity of each project.

The projects are provided under the license stated in each repository. They do not include a promise of hosted service, prepared regional data, model calibration, operational support, or feature parity with any commercial product.

## UrbanSim Inc.

UrbanSim Inc. is a separate commercial company founded by Paul Waddell. It provides products and professional services for organizations that require an operational modeling program rather than a self-managed software library. These offerings may include:

- curated and maintained regional data;
- model specification, estimation, calibration, and validation;
- managed cloud computation and production operations;
- scenario management, collaboration, mapping, and visualization;
- integration with client systems and other modeling platforms;
- training, service levels, and continuing technical support; and
- proprietary or separately licensed technology developed by the company.

Commercial products may use UDST components, but they also include data, workflows, interfaces, infrastructure, and technology that are not part of the open-source repositories.

## How the boundary is managed

UDST will maintain credible, useful open-source foundations. Compatibility fixes, packaging, tests, documentation, security work, and improvements to established capabilities are normal open-source maintenance. A proposed addition that creates a major new subsystem, reproduces separately developed commercial technology, or imposes a substantial continuing maintenance obligation requires a specific product and governance review before it is placed on a public roadmap.

The fact that a capability can be built from public methods does not obligate UDST or UrbanSim Inc. to develop or maintain it in an open-source repository. Conversely, commercial considerations do not alter the license or availability of code already released.

Funding open-source work does not purchase ownership of UDST, control of its roadmap, or automatic acceptance of a contribution. Sponsors and users may propose and fund work; UDST maintainers decide whether it belongs in an official repository under the same technical and provenance standards applied to other contributions.

## Attribution

Use of a UDST project does not imply endorsement by UDST, Paul Waddell, or UrbanSim Inc. Inclusion of an organization or project in an applications list does not imply that it is a customer. Commercial customers should be identified as such only when public attribution is permitted.

UrbanSim Inc. may describe its support for UDST and its own products accurately, but should not suggest that community contributors or public research sponsors endorse the company.

## Choosing an approach

Organizations that have their own technical team and want to inspect, adapt, and operate the modeling software may use the UDST repositories directly. Organizations seeking prepared data, a managed platform, production implementation, calibration, visualization, or continuing support may work with UrbanSim Inc. Neither route changes the rights granted by the open-source licenses.
